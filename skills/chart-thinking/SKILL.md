# Chart thinking

The user is training their brain to think in models and mathematically. Qualifying moments become a **rep** — a short training repetition built around a chart. The chart is the interface, not decoration.

## The rep

1. Pause at the moment (a decision, a tradeoff, a mechanism, a recurring pattern).
2. Ask the user to predict the model's shape before revealing anything: "what shape does this relationship have?"
3. Reveal the chart and name the form out loud (inverted-U, DAG, phase diagram, convex hull…).
4. Connect both altitudes: big picture (what family of situations this shape governs) and detail (the mechanism that produces the shape).

"I don't know" is a valid prediction — reveal, teach the mechanism, move on. A rep ends when the form has been named and both altitudes stated.

## Dosage

- Full reps: 2–4 per working day. Training value follows an inverted-U in reps per day — past the peak, charts become wallpaper (habituation) and marginal value goes negative. Spend reps on high-leverage moments only.
- Cheap naming: uncapped. Name models in passing ("that's a feedback loop") with no ritual and usually no drawing.
- A chart must earn its place: no structure, no chart. Saying so out loud is itself modeling the rule.

## Vocabulary

Anchored to the scientific diagram collection at https://diagrams.janosh.dev/ (~160 diagrams: physics, machine learning, math, CS, fractals). Each full rep uses at least one form from the collection, picked by **constrained randomness**: shortlist the forms that genuinely relate to the moment (see [JANOSH-CATALOG.md](JANOSH-CATALOG.md)), then pick randomly within the shortlist. The randomness is interleaving (mixed practice — sticks better than blocked drilling); the shortlist keeps picks from being nonsense. Never pure random over the whole collection, never a fixed deck.

## Interaction

Make a chart live (sliders, clickable parts) only when the model has a natural knob and twisting it teaches something. Otherwise a still picture.

## Rendering

Best available medium, in order: the harness's inline chart widget → mermaid or inline SVG → a published artifact page.

## Language

Vocabulary is part of the training. Keep the technical terms, gloss each in place — "fluency (= fast, without effort)" — and teach terms the user may not know. Chart names count as vocabulary; say them every time.
